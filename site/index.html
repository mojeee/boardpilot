What it does

From the idea to a working board.

One app for the whole job: plan the project, wire it, flash it, find what is wrong and learn why. Every feature works in simulator mode too, so you can try all of it without a board.

1Build by chatting

Describe it. Get parts and wiring that pass the checks.

Say what you want to make. The assistant asks only what it needs, compares parts with you, then proposes the parts, the wires, what else to buy and starter code. Nothing changes until you press Apply.

  • Parts from the open library, pins from the board’s own rules
  • Every proposal runs through the same wiring checker as your own drawing
  • Tested on 26 real requests, display designs included: 26 of 26 passed with Claude Sonnet 5.5 (23 of 23 with Sonnet 5)
Watch a recorded conversation → Read the test reports
2Display Studio

See what your screen will show, before you have it.

Describe the screens you want, or ask for test patterns. The app draws them pixel for pixel at your display’s real resolution and colours, runs them with simulated values, then writes the Arduino code for your wiring.

  • 22 display modules: SSD1306 and SH1106 OLEDs, ST7789, ST7735, ILI9341 and the round GC9A01, e-paper and more
  • Text, values, bars, graphs, gauges, icons, test patterns and blinking warnings, with timed or conditional screens
  • The generated code is built with the real Adafruit GFX library and compared pixel by pixel with the preview
Watch the test-pattern conversation →
3See inside the board

Every pin, wire and part, live in 3D.

The board is built from its datasheet, so every pin is real: click it to see what it can do, what to avoid and its live level. Wires glow when the bus talks.

  • Wiring checked as you draw: flash pins, 5 V on a 3.3 V part, crossed buses, missing ground
  • The same project as a 2D pinout, a wiring diagram and a schematic
  • Click any log line or AI answer to point the camera at its pin
Try it live in your browser →
4Debug with real measurements

From “it doesn’t work” to the exact wire.

Tell it what is wrong. BoardPilot checks the pull-ups, scans the bus as wired and with SDA and SCL swapped, reads the chip ID, and tells you the cause with the evidence.

  • Every answer is labelled: measured, from a datasheet, or a suggestion
  • Guided fixes for a board that is not found, keeps resetting or prints garbage
  • One click writes a report to share
Watch the debugging demo →
5Flash safely

Your old program is backed up first.

BoardPilot only reads by default. Before the first write it saves the whole flash, and anything that writes to the board waits for your click.

  • Full flash backup before the first write, restored with one click
  • The AI can ask to write, but only you can confirm
  • Refuses flash pins and outputs on input-only pins, even in firmware
  • Never shows a voltage it did not measure

  • Sensor registers read from the chip and explained bit by bit

How it works

How it works, and how you can be sure.

Five steps from an idea to a working board. For each one: what BoardPilot does, and how you can check it yourself instead of taking our word for it.

The technical details: board files, pin choice, every wiring rule, the AI tools, the simulator →

  1. 1Choosing the parts

    Tell the assistant what you want to build. It asks only what changes the design, such as the power supply or indoors or outdoors, one question at a time. When there is a real choice, it shows the options as cards that say what is different: what they measure, the voltage, the wiring and the known traps.

    How you can be sure
    • It can only use parts from the open library: 385 parts, each with its pins, voltage, I2C addresses and known traps, and their sources.
    • A part that is not in the library is refused, never invented. If the library cannot do what you ask, it says so.
    • You make the choice, and nothing is added until you press Apply.
    Watch it choose the parts for a plant waterer →

  2. 2Wiring

    Pins are picked by the board’s own rules, written from its datasheet. The assistant draws the wires, adds part-to-part wires for loads such as a pump on a MOSFET module, and lists what else to buy.

    How you can be sure
    • Every proposal goes through the wiring checker before you see it: flash pins, input-only pins, strapping pins, ADC2 with Wi-Fi, 5 V parts on 3.3 V pins, crossed I2C, two parts at the same I2C address, missing power or ground. Errors must be fixed first.
    • Pull-ups, voltage dividers, level shifters and flyback diodes go on the shopping list from the parts’ own library notes.
    • Nothing changes until you press Apply, and Undo takes it back. Wires you draw yourself are checked by the same rules.
    Try it: draw a wire in the live demo →

  3. 3Debugging

    With your OK, BoardPilot installs a small diagnostic agent on the board, after backing up your program. Then it measures: the pull-ups on the I2C pins, a bus scan as wired and with SDA and SCL swapped, and the chip’s ID register.

    How you can be sure
    • Every fact has a label: Measured (in this session, with the tool that measured it), From documentation (with the datasheet section) or Suggestion (you confirm it before anything relies on it).
    • Crossed wires are shown by a measurement, not a guess: the sensor answers only when SDA and SCL are exchanged.
    • It never shows a voltage it did not measure. On pins without an ADC it reports only HIGH or LOW.
    Watch the debugging demo →

  4. 4Code and flashing

    BoardPilot writes starter code from your drawing, using only the pins you wired, for Arduino, the Pico SDK, ESP-IDF or STM32 HAL. You can also open your own sketch in the Code panel.

    How you can be sure
    • The code checker compares the code with the wiring as you type: I2C pins in the wrong order, a serial speed that does not match the monitor, an LED on another pin. Each finding marks its line, and simple ones have a one-click Fix.
    • Code from the AI goes through the same checker before it is shown, and waits for you to accept it.
    • Flashing needs your confirmation. Before the first write the whole flash is backed up, and one click puts your old program back.
    See the code checker in the live demo →

  5. 5Simulation

    Simulator mode runs the whole app without a board: the same screens and checks, on a virtual board with realistic faults such as crossed wires, a fake sensor, brownouts or the wrong baud rate. Template projects run step by step, with the running line highlighted and the value of every part and pin beside the code.

    How you can be sure
    • Simulated values are always labelled “simulated”, so you never mistake them for measurements.
    • The simulated board works through the same interface as a real one, so the steps you learn are the steps you will use.
    • Display Studio shows what your display module will draw, pixel by pixel, before you have it, and a test checks that the generated code draws exactly the same pixels with the real Adafruit GFX library.
    Run a template in the simulator →

Where the others are the better choice

  • Writing codeThe Arduino IDE and PlatformIO are full code editors with autocompletion, library managers and, on supported chips, a step-through debugger. BoardPilot’s code panel checks your sketch against the wiring, but it is not a full IDE: keep your editor and use both.
  • Simulating in the browserWokwi runs your code on simulated boards right in the browser, with nothing to install, and has plugins for VS Code and JetBrains.
  • Designing a PCBFritzing goes from a breadboard view to a schematic to a PCB layout for production. BoardPilot does not design PCBs.
  • Any questionA general chatbot can talk about almost anything. BoardPilot’s assistant is narrower: it works on your project, with the measurements from your board and the datasheet sections, and says which is which.

FAQ

Questions

Is BoardPilot open source?

It is source-available. All the code is public on GitHub under the BoardPilot License: you can read, study, modify and build it. Using it after the 30-day trial needs a license key. The firmware (diagnostic agent and BoardPilotProbe library) is MIT licensed.

I am a complete beginner. Where do I start?

Start with the free lessons on this site: what a microcontroller is, then electronics basics, GPIO, buses, ADC and timers. You do not need a board: the app’s simulator (and the browser demo on this page) lets you wire parts and see what happens. When you get a board, an ESP32 DevKit or a Raspberry Pi Pico is a good first choice, and the app’s hands-on labs check your wiring with real measurements.

How is BoardPilot different from the Arduino IDE, PlatformIO or Wokwi?

Those are tools for writing code (Arduino IDE, PlatformIO) or simulating it in the browser (Wokwi), and they do that very well. BoardPilot sits next to them and looks at the board: it checks your wiring against the pin rules, measures pins and buses on the real board, backs up the flash before any write and explains each result with its source. Many people write code in their usual editor and use BoardPilot to wire, check and debug.

Do the demos on this page call an AI?

The recorded conversations do not: they are real conversations with Claude, recorded once and replayed in your browser. In the live demo, the assistant answers with the free demo AI only when you ask it something. Everything else in the demos, including the checks and the simulated board, runs in your browser.

Which boards does it support?

ESP32 DevKit, ESP32-S3 and ESP32-C3 DevKits, Raspberry Pi Pico, Pico W and Pico 2, Arduino Uno, Nano and Mega, STM32 Nucleo-F401RE and Black Pill, Nordic nRF52840 DK and Teensy 4.1. Boards are data files, so more are easy to add.

My computer warns that the app is from an unknown developer.

The early builds are not signed yet. On Mac, right-click BoardPilot in Applications and choose Open (or System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway). On Windows, click More info → Run anyway in the SmartScreen window.

Do I need a board to try it?

No. Simulator mode runs the whole app with a virtual board and realistic faults: crossed wires, a fake BME280, brownouts, wrong baud rate.

Does it need the internet or an AI account?

No. Checks, measurements and the simulator work offline. The AI assistant is optional: try it with the free demo (a limited, older Gemini model, for testing only) or use your own key for Claude, GPT or Gemini.

What does it install on my board?

Only when you confirm: a small diagnostic agent that lets the app read pins and run bus tests. Your program is backed up first and comes back with one click.

Can my AI coding agent use it?

Yes, since 0.6. BoardPilot runs a local MCP server (off by default, localhost only). Claude Code, Cursor and Claude Desktop can then identify the board, scan I2C, read pins and check wiring and code. Every result says whether it was measured or read from a datasheet, and nothing is written to the board without your click in the app.

Can I learn embedded with it?

Yes. The app has 11 visual lessons, from what a microcontroller is to RTOS and a senior roadmap, with interactive demos. Labs are checked on your board (or in the simulator), three portfolio projects are built stage by stage, and an interview coach helps you practise.
